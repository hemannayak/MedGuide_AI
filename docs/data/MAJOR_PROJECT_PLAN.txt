MedGuide AI — Major Project Plan

Build the verified knowledge base and symptom checker together, then use
experiments to choose models. Keep the existing architecture and improve it
incrementally.

1. Define what the symptom checker supports

List the symptom groups, patient populations, and information needed, such as
duration, severity, and associated symptoms. Establish where the system can
provide guidance, request clarification, or escalate.

2. Collect and review authoritative documents

Select relevant MoHFW, ICMR, and WHO publications. Record versions,
applicability, permissions, and exact source sections. The proposed 40–60
documents is a planning estimate; coverage determines the final number.

3. Build two separate medical resources

- RAG knowledge base: reviewed passages for explanations and citations.
- Triage rule register: source-backed conditions and escalation actions.

The model must not invent medical rules.

4. Prepare the research datasets

Use eligible IHQID examples for healthcare intent/entity understanding,
permitted MedQuAD subsets for consumer QA, and IndicMSMARCO for general
multilingual retrieval evaluation. Additional reviewed symptom-specific
examples are needed because these datasets do not provide a complete triage
checker.

5. Compare models in isolation

- Existing embeddings versus IndicSBERT and E5-small for retrieval.
- MuRIL for intent/entity extraction after suitable training data is available.
- IndicTrans2 for local Hindi/Telugu query translation.

Keep separate indexes for different embedding models. Measure English, Hindi,
and Telugu independently.

6. Connect the symptom-checking flow

Patient description
    -> Extract symptoms and relevant context
    -> Clarify missing or ambiguous information
    -> Apply documented safety rules
    -> Escalate, or retrieve supported general guidance
    -> Validate the response and show sources

7. Evaluate before promoting changes

Measure missed red flags, false alarms, extraction errors, retrieval relevance,
citation correctness, unsupported claims, translation errors, latency, and
memory. Include negation, indirect descriptions, code-mixing, and unavailable
services.

8. Integrate successful candidates into the full platform

Connect patient records, healthcare-worker review, alerts, and follow-ups.
Verify the complete workflow.

Current status

The shortlist and adoption plan are recorded. Downloads, dataset preparation,
model comparisons, and runtime integration for this shortlist have not happened
yet.

Next step

The first source identity/currency/rights audit and symptom coverage map are now
recorded. All four local PDF hashes match the registry, but that alone does not
establish clinical validity or permission for reuse.

Findings: the local PEN file is a 2018 training publication despite its 2020
filename; the malaria edition is superseded by the WHO September 2026 edition;
the ICMR document states prior permission for reproduction/transmission; and
existing symptom rules need exact source sections and applicability mapping.

Next: review exact emergency/respiratory source sections and current malaria
material, resolve source permissions/metadata, and prepare source-linked labels.
