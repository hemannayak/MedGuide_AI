# WHO Essential Primary Health Care and Clinical Guidelines (2023)

## Section 1: General Symptom Management - Fever and Hyperthermia
Primary care providers should evaluate patients presenting with acute fever for underlying infections. For mild to moderate fever without severe red-flag symptoms, initial home care includes adequate hydration, rest, and physical cooling measures. Oral paracetamol (500mg to 1000mg every 4 to 6 hours, maximum 4000mg per 24 hours) may be administered for symptom relief. Avoid aspirin in children due to the risk of Reye's syndrome.

## Section 2: Acute Respiratory Infection Guidelines
Patients presenting with mild upper respiratory tract symptoms, cough, and sore throat without severe shortness of breath should be managed with supportive care. Adequate fluid intake, steam inhalation, and throat gargles are recommended. If high fever persists beyond 3 days, or if chest pain, severe difficulty breathing, or bluish discoloration of lips occurs, immediate escalation to a secondary healthcare facility is mandatory.

## Section 3: Acute Gastroenteritis and Dehydration
In cases of acute watery diarrhea and vomiting, primary management centers on preventing and treating dehydration using Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS). Administer 200ml to 400ml of ORS solution after each loose stool. Continue normal age-appropriate feeding. Zinc supplementation (20mg daily for 14 days) is recommended for pediatric diarrhea. Avoid anti-motility agents in pediatric patients or cases of dysentery with bloody stool.
