# MoHFW Standard Treatment Guidelines for Primary Health Centres (2022)

## Section 1: Rural Primary Care & Dengue Management
In endemic rural areas, acute high fever accompanied by severe headache, retro-orbital pain, myalgia, and arthralgia should raise suspicion for Dengue or Malaria. Paracetamol is the sole antipyretic recommended for fever management in Dengue. Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) such as ibuprofen or diclofenac are strictly contraindicated due to the risk of severe bleeding and platelet dysfunction.

## Section 2: Red-Flag Emergency Triage Criteria for Rural Health Workers
Healthcare workers and AI triage systems must identify predefined red-flag symptoms for immediate emergency transfer:
1. Severe chest pain radiating to jaw or left arm.
2. Sudden onset of severe dyspnea (shortness of breath) or respiratory failure.
3. Altered mental status, confusion, or loss of consciousness.
4. Severe abdominal rigidity or uncontrollable hemorrhage.
5. Persistent vomiting preventing oral hydration in young infants or elderly patients.
When red-flag symptoms are identified, immediately issue an emergency alert and arrange transport to the nearest Community Health Centre (CHC) or District Hospital.

## Section 3: Essential Hypertension and Diabetes Guidelines
Screen adult patients for hypertension during primary clinic visits. Persistent blood pressure >= 140/90 mmHg requires lifestyle modification (sodium restriction < 2g/day, regular physical activity) and clinical follow-up. For type 2 diabetes mellitus, first-line management includes dietary counseling, exercise, and metformin administration under clinical supervision. Regular monitoring of fasting blood glucose and HbA1c is essential for long-term control.
