### ABSTRACT

Access to timely, reliable, and linguistically accessible primary healthcare guidance remains an acute operational challenge for rural and underserved communities across India. In these regions, geographical distance from accredited secondary and tertiary clinical centers, severe deficits in the local healthcare workforce, low functional and digital health literacy, and profound linguistic diversity frequently converge to delay vital medical attention. When early or ambiguous symptoms emerge, rural residents are often forced to choose between navigating commercial search engines that induce catastrophic anxiety (cyberchondria), traveling long distances at high out-of-pocket costs for minor self-limiting conditions, or ignoring early manifestations of acute, life-threatening medical crises. Furthermore, standard commercial artificial intelligence solutions present severe operational liabilities: their parametric neural architectures are vulnerable to stochastic hallucinations, they expose sensitive Protected Health Information (PHI) to third-party commercial cloud APIs, and their monolithic English orientation alienates non-English-speaking populations.

To resolve these systemic barriers, this BTech Major Project introduces **MedGuide AI: An AI-Powered Rural Healthcare Intelligence and Digital Care Platform**. MedGuide AI establishes a comprehensive, safety-first digital care ecosystem that bridges the clinical divide across web and mobile form factors while strictly operating as an assistive, evidence-grounded decision-support tool rather than an autonomous medical practitioner. The platform natively supports English, Hindi, and Telugu across both textual and speech-driven modalities.

The architectural foundation of MedGuide AI is organized into a dual-tiered, decoupled safety pipeline. At the front line of patient ingestion, an upstream **Deterministic Red-Flag Safety Gate** evaluates narrative symptom descriptions against an evidence-based clinical ontology within sub-15-millisecond latency. Critical clinical emergencies—including acute coronary syndromes, acute cerebrovascular accidents (FAST protocol), severe respiratory compromise, acute anaphylaxis, and surgical abdomens—are deterministically intercepted. The system bypasses all downstream generative models to issue immediate emergency directives, localized emergency telephone contacts (112, 108, 102), and first-aid stabilization protocols.

For non-emergent health inquiries, the platform routes user concerns through a high-performance **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)** pipeline. Clinical reference documents—curated from authoritative public health bodies including the World Health Organization (WHO) and the Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR)—are segmented into semantic chunks and mapped into 384-dimensional dense vector embeddings using `paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2`. Semantic nearest-neighbor searches are executed within an on-premise PostgreSQL instance equipped with the `pgvector` extension utilizing Hierarchical Navigable Small World (HNSW) geometric graphs. A strict evidence sufficiency gate enforces that if retrieved literature fails to satisfy a cosine similarity threshold ($\ge 0.70$), the system executes a safe refusal rather than confabulating speculative guidance. Grounded natural language synthesis is executed either locally via Meta’s Llama 3.2 (3B) / Qwen (4B) running under Ollama with rigid JSON Schema output enforcement, or optionally via dedicated Sarvam AI conversation endpoints, providing empathetic, layperson-accessible summaries paired with verifiable document-level citations.

Expanding substantially beyond conversational triage, the Major Project implements a holistic continuum of primary care:
1. **Prescription Understanding & OCR Pipeline**: A native camera and file-upload pipeline that preprocesses prescription photographs, performs Optical Character Recognition (OCR), extracts candidate pharmaceutical entities (medicine name, dosage, frequency, duration, administration instructions) using heuristic NLP rules, and holds them in a reviewable draft state, strictly requiring human-in-the-loop verification before scheduling.
2. **Medication Scheduling & Adherence Tracking**: A deterministic scheduling engine that maps verified prescriptions into structured dosage timelines, dispatches local reminders, and logs longitudinal adherence events (`TAKEN`, `MISSED`, `SKIPPED`).
3. **Longitudinal Health Timeline**: A unified, chronological patient record that consolidates self-reported symptom episodes, triage classifications, verified medications, and clinical notes into an auditable health journey.
4. **Healthcare Worker (HCW) Collaboration Portal**: A specialized clinical dashboard that empowers Accredited Social Health Activists (ASHA), Auxiliary Nurse Midwives (ANM), and primary care clinicians to review assigned patient rosters, inspect AI-generated symptom summaries, monitor emergency alerts, and manage scheduled home follow-up visits.
5. **Cross-Platform Client Ecosystem**: A responsive 52-route Next.js 16 web application paired with a dedicated 22-screen React Native / Expo SDK 57 mobile application featuring native hardware audio recording, camera ingestion, secure credential storage (`expo-secure-store`), and pedometer/wellness telemetry (`expo-sensors`).

The Stage 1 development establishes the comprehensive requirements, multi-tier system architecture, verified database schemas (22 relational and vector entities), RESTful API specifications (13 route groups), deterministic triage rule ontologies, and cross-platform interactive prototypes. MedGuide AI demonstrates that by uniting deterministic clinical safety, multilingual speech processing, local vector retrieval, and human-in-the-loop care workflows, digital healthcare platforms can deliver safe, private, and equitable healthcare intelligence to rural and underserved populations without compromising clinical safety or data sovereignty.
