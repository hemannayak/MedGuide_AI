2 LITERATURE REVIEW AND RELATED WORK
MedGuide AI combines accessible interaction, medical-source retrieval and explicit safety boundaries. The related work is therefore reviewed across retrieval-augmented generation, sentence embeddings, healthcare AI governance and local model execution. The literature motivates engineering choices; it does not establish the clinical validity of this project.
2.1 Retrieval augmented generation
Lewis et al. [1] describe a generation architecture that combines a parametric language model with retrieved non-parametric knowledge. Their work demonstrates the value of retrieval for knowledge-intensive NLP tasks. For MedGuide, the relevant principle is that external evidence can be retrieved before an answer is generated, allowing the knowledge collection to be maintained separately from the model. The project adapts this principle to medical documents stored in PostgreSQL. Its evidence gate refuses a response when no acceptable chunk is retrieved. Retrieval can improve traceability, but it cannot guarantee that the retrieved passage is suitable for a particular patient or that every generated statement follows from the passage.
2.2 Sentence embeddings and multilingual retrieval
Sentence-BERT [2] uses sentence-level representations for semantic comparison and retrieval. MiniLM [3] studies transformer compression through attention distillation. These are relevant foundations for a compact embedding component. The model card for paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2 [4] identifies a 384-dimensional sentence representation and multilingual support. MedGuide selects this identifier in knowledge_service.py and checks the resulting vector length. The model must be downloaded and loaded successfully; encoding failure is treated as unavailable evidence rather than replaced by invented vectors. General multilingual model support is not proof of reliable Hindi or Telugu medical retrieval; language-specific testing remains necessary.
2.3 Healthcare AI ethics and governance
WHO guidance on large multimodal models in health [5] discusses risks involving inaccurate outputs, bias, privacy and inappropriate reliance on AI. These considerations support MedGuide’s informational scope and professional-care boundary. The implementation includes refusal responses and deterministic checks before generation. Nevertheless, a rule list or prompt instruction is not a clinical safety certification. The project needs documented rule provenance, medical review and evaluation of false negatives, false positives and adversarial inputs before real patient use.
2.4 Medical sources and provenance
The repository includes four medical PDFs and a source register: WHO hypertension guidance, WHO PEN material, WHO malaria guidance and an ICMR paediatric/extrapulmonary tuberculosis workflow. The hypertension guideline [6] is a primary publication; the PEN package [7] concerns essential noncommunicable disease interventions. Such materials are knowledge inputs, not evidence that MedGuide is endorsed by their publishers. Page numbers, section text, publisher and source URL are retained with chunks. Publication version, licence and document review must remain traceable throughout ingestion.
2.5 Vector storage and local inference
The pgvector project [8] provides vector similarity search inside PostgreSQL. MedGuide uses its cosine-distance operator with 384-dimensional vectors, keeping source documents and chunks in the same relational system as application data. Ollama [9] provides a local model interface; its chat API accepts messages and returns generated content. MedGuide’s gateway supports Ollama, optional hosted inference and a mock provider. The configured Llama 3.2 3B candidate is an integration choice requiring hardware and language evaluation, not a final medically validated model.

2.6 Gap addressed by MedGuide
The project targets the combination of rural accessibility, familiar-language interaction, visible evidence and a constrained informational role. The practical contribution is an integrated engineering prototype: a responsive interface, authenticated application routes, a relational care-data model and a retrieval/safety service boundary. Novel clinical performance, deployment effectiveness and user satisfaction have not been established. Comparisons with commercial symptom checkers or telemedicine platforms should be based on separately verified product evidence rather than assumptions about their language support, fees or capabilities.
3 PURPOSE AND SCOPE
3.1 Purpose
The purpose is to provide an understandable first layer of healthcare information for rural and underserved communities. Distance, language and the difficulty of judging online information motivate a simple interface for asking questions, structuring symptoms and reviewing available evidence. The platform supports conversations with qualified healthcare professionals; it must not independently diagnose, prescribe or change treatment.
The engineering objective is to demonstrate how frontend design, REST APIs, authentication, relational storage, document processing, multilingual embeddings and local inference can be composed with explicit safety boundaries. Development uses synthetic information and an auditable source collection. The mini-project emphasises text interaction and preliminary symptom workflows rather than a claim of complete clinical deployment.

4.3 Development
FastAPI endpoints delegate work to services and receive SQLAlchemy sessions through dependencies. User accounts are associated with roles and patient profiles. def hash_password(password: str) -> str:
    """Hash a plaintext password securely using bcrypt."""
    pwd_bytes = password.encode("utf-8")
    salt = bcrypt.gensalt()
    return bcrypt.hashpw(pwd_bytes, salt).decode("utf-8")
def verify_password(plain_password: str, hashed_password: str) -> bool:
    """Verify a plaintext password against a stored bcrypt hash."""

def chunk_extracted_pages(
    pages: List[Dict[str, Any]],
    chunk_size: int = 500,
    overlap: int = 50,
) -> List[Dict[str, Any]]:
    """Section-aware semantic chunking with exact provenance preservation."""
    chunks = []
    chunk_index = 0
    for page in pages:
        page_num = page["page_number"]
        section_title = page["section_title"]
        text = page["text"]
        start = 0
        while start < len(text):
            end = start + chunk_size
            chunk_text = text[start:end]
            if len(chunk_text.strip()) > 30:
                chunks.append({
                    "chunk_index": chunk_index,
                    "page_number": page_num,
                    "section_title": section_title,
                    "text": chunk_text.strip(),
                })
                chunk_index += 1
            start += (chunk_size - overlap)
    return chunks

    """Lazy load SentenceTransformer multilingual embedding model."""
    global _embedding_model

def generate_embedding(text: str) -> List[float]:
    """Generate 384-dimensional vector embedding for input text."""
    model = get_embedding_model()
    if model is not None:
        try:

    response_text: str,

    Returns (is_valid, failure_reason).

        if pattern_re.search(response_text):
            return False, reason
    return True, None

8.1 PostgreSQL and pgvector configuration
SQLAlchemy uses the configured PostgreSQL URL and creates a request-scoped session. Alembic provides migrations. KnowledgeChunk declares Vector(384), while the retrieval query uses cosine distance. Referential constraints connect care records to patient profiles and knowledge chunks to their documents. The health probe checks connectivity, not completeness of schema, indexing or clinical knowledge ingestion.

8.2 Users and patient profiles table
Table | Key fields | Relationship
users | id, role_id, login_identifier, password_hash, status | Role foreign key and unique login identifier
patient_profiles | id, user_id, display_name, preferred_language, date_of_birth | Unique user_id; patient-owned care records
roles | id, name | Patient, healthcare-worker and administrator role concepts
A patient profile stores demographic and language information separately from authentication credentials. Password hashes are never presentation fields. Optional data should only be collected when required, and authorisation must restrict access to the appropriate patient or authorised healthcare worker. The actual backend fields must be distinguished from frontend-only sample fields; for example, patient_profiles does not define every village, allergy or health-note field used in a preview.
8.3 Symptom records table
symptom_records stores its patient foreign key, input source, raw input reference, structured JSON data and report timestamp. The symptom service also creates a timeline event and can create an alert during analysis. JSONB supports structured narrative metadata while patient identity and ownership remain relational. Repeated analysis and alert duplication deserve explicit idempotency tests before deployment.
8.4 Conversations and messages table
conversations stores patient ownership, language, status and start/end timestamps. conversation_messages stores sender type, text, optional metadata and creation time. The chat service filters supplied conversation IDs by patient ownership. Only recent turns are added to prompt context; they are not treated as authoritative medical evidence. Retention and deletion policies must address the sensitivity of conversation text.
8.5 Medical documents and knowledge chunks table
Entity | Stored fields | Design purpose
medical_documents | title, publisher, URL, publication date, version, language, review status | Document provenance and review lifecycle
knowledge_chunks | document_id, chunk_index, content, embedding, metadata | Page/section-linked text and semantic retrieval
The schema permits licence, publication date and version metadata, but the current ingestion function does not populate all of those document-level fields supplied by the corpus manifest. The report therefore does not assume that every row has complete provenance. PDF filename and source-register claims should be verified against the document itself. Successful ingestion must be measured from database records rather than inferred from files being present.

9 CONCLUSION
MedGuide AI demonstrates a structured engineering approach to rural healthcare information access. Its web interface, application routes and backend modules bring together authentication, patient-owned records, deterministic matching, document provenance and retrieval-assisted generation. The two-laptop separation keeps frontend development independent from database and model infrastructure, while configurable service layers prepare integration.
The most important design decision is to place boundaries around generated healthcare content. Emergency paths bypass generative inference, unavailable embeddings fail closed, insufficient evidence produces refusal and responses can expose source metadata. These controls reduce specific engineering failure modes but do not eliminate hallucination, prove clinical safety or validate a diagnosis. Keyword rules, retrieval thresholds, multilingual handling and output validation need further expert review and systematic measurement.
The current evidence supports the claim of an implemented software prototype and tested frontend behaviours. It does not support reported clinical accuracy, quantified rural health outcomes, a fixed verified chunk count, complete offline operation or an already validated APK. Next steps are retrieval calibration, traceable rule review, secure API integration, measured language quality, improved evidence messaging and supervised usability studies. Speech, OCR and care-continuity enhancements should follow the approved scope and human review requirements.
